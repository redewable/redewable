This page traces the transmission-service route as a parallel workstream — engineered, priced, and proceeding while the campus is built behind the meter. Topology

Generation-served, battery in-series, grid available.

On-site generation remains the carrying source in every configuration — the grid tie adds to it, not the other way around. Once the tie is energized, utility service from Phantom Hill becomes an additional supply into the campus: it lands at the LST point of interconnection, passes the metering boundary into the ReDew campus substation, and reaches the in-series long-duration battery and the data center load. Just as important, the tie opens the battery’s discharge path to market — the export revenue the injection study confirms the bus can absorb.

Interconnection engineering by EPE (Electric Power Engineers), working directly with Lone Star Transmission, the transmission service provider at Phantom Hill. Facilities scope and ownership boundary are set by LST through the Facilities Interconnection Study. Specialized regulatory counsel will provide an independent legal opinion on the configuration as part of tenant and capital diligence.

Sequence

The grid tie, running in parallel.

Feasibility and transfer-limit studies are complete, EPE is engaged, and the Phase 1 application is drafted and preparing to file.

01
Application to LST
Under the small-load threshold, the Phase 1 request stays with the TSP and never enters the ERCOT Batch process — a path confirmed in writing. EPE prepares and carries the filing.
02
System Impact Study
The utility studies the new load against current base cases — thermal, voltage, and stability at the bus — and identifies any network upgrades.
03
Facilities Interconnection Study
The utility scopes the interconnection facilities — terminal equipment, protection, metering, and the ownership boundary at the POI — on a defined commercial timeline.
04
Signed Position → Grid Service
A signed position brings utility service to the campus. Phase 1 does not wait on it — NTP is gated by the air permit, equipment mobilization and construction financing. The 600 MW envelope for Phases 2–4 files as one ERCOT Batch application when scale warrants.

Why the route matters: new large-load interconnections in ERCOT commonly take five to seven years, behind more than 400 GW of requests. Phase 1 doesn’t stand in that line — and that is the difference between talking about 2028 and delivering it.

Feasibility and transfer-limit studies were commissioned by ReDewable and its partners on publicly available ERCOT SSWG base cases. No interconnection application has been filed to date.
