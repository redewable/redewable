The Asset

Grid capacity at scale, next door to the largest AI compute build in North America.

ReDew Anson holds the envelope at the in-service Phantom Hill 345 kV substation, with site control established across 165 acres. We're developing it as an AI-grade data center plus long-duration energy campus — vanadium flow storage in-series between grid and load, owned by the project and paid inside the rent; on-site generation funded and operated by an independent power producer and passed through at cost; each phase financed at commercial operation with the 40% investment tax credit and an infrastructure term loan — anchored by a data center tenant. Phase 1 (74.5 MW of critical IT) leads; three 200 MW phases follow in 2029–2031 for about 700 MW of campus in all. Phase 1 is powered behind the meter and needs no interconnection application to build or energize — with a fast sub-75 MW small-load tie to Lone Star Transmission proceeding in parallel: no ERCOT Batch process, no queue.

Capacity is the bottleneck. We hold 165 acres at an in-service 345 kV bus studied to 2 GW — next to the corridor that needs it most.

Investment Thesis

Four facts the bet rests on.

i.
We don't stand in the queue.
More than 400 GW of large-load requests sit in the ERCOT queue. Phase 1 never joins it — the campus runs behind the meter on IPP-owned generation and needs no interconnection application to energize. A sub-75 MW small-load tie with the transmission service provider is proceeding in parallel on roughly a nine-month cycle, confirmed in writing. Power that doesn't wait is the moat.
ii.
AI compute has set the clock.
The Stargate AI campus near Abilene sits ~19 miles south. Hyperscalers and AI infrastructure operators are reserving capacity today for energization windows two-plus years out. The buyer is already in market.
iii.
LDUPS stacks three revenue streams.
Battery in-series captures ERCOT arbitrage, sells ancillary services, and firms the tenant's power — from one asset position. Hold-and-operate lets the operator capture all three. Develop-and-sell would surrender most of it.
iv.
Phased monetization derisks the bet.
Phase 1 — 74.5 MW of billable critical IT on a modular, prefabricated build — is independently financeable and monetizable, followed by three ~200 MW phases inside the 600 MW studied envelope. Phase 1 economics close before full-envelope COD. No single-build cliff.

Structure

The project company. Paid first. Every phase.

i.
ReDew Anson, LLC — the project company
Investors hold Class B units in the Texas entity that holds the whole Anson position — site control, studies, permits, counterparty relationships, and every phase — the same entity a buyer would acquire. ReDewable Energy Company serves as Managing Member with full operational control; the units are passive. Commitments fund about 81% at close — the land closing first; the balance is callable only at three defined gates.
ii.
Paid-first waterfall
At a sale or financing of the position: drawn capital back, then a preferred return that accrues only on drawn capital, then a participation in net proceeds — all before any distribution to the Managing Member. If no monetization event occurs within 36 months, the commitment converts to common equity in the project company. Project financing — ITC transferability, the infrastructure term loan, construction debt, vendor credit — enters as credits or debt, not equity.
iii.
Every phase, not just the first
Because the units sit in the project company, the investors’ interest attaches to Phase 1 and to the three ~200 MW phases inside the 600 MW studied envelope alike — including the developer fee, pro rata share and promote the company keeps when each phase is financed with a construction-equity partner — with pre-emptive rights on any new equity issued by the company.

Permanent financing for each phase closes at commercial operation: an infrastructure term loan sized on debt-service coverage, loan-to-cost and loan-to-value (modeled at 8% over 20 years; $996M at Phase 1), the 40% investment tax credit on the project-owned battery and grid-side works ($170M net at Phase 1), and permanent equity of roughly $304M that stays in the phase company. C-PACE, carried in earlier versions of this page, is no longer in the base case: Jones County has not adopted a PACE program, and the Company has been advised that a C-PACE lender active in data centers would more likely offer an infrastructure loan at this size. Through construction the battery is proposed to be carried on TerraFlow vendor credit and paid at COD. Later phases are financed at the phase-company level with a construction-equity partner under a standard developer fee + promote structure. None of it is committed.

Reg D Rule 506(b), accredited investors only; $50K minimum commitment. Preferred-return rate, participation, and conversion terms are stated in the Offering Memorandum and are subject to securities-counsel review.
